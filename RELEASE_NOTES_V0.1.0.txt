OPEN FRONTIER — V0.1.0 "STARS & SKIES"
===============================================

The first major release for Open Frontier is here, and it's all about
the sky, and ALOT of bug fixes.

EVERY SECTOR HAS A SUN NOW
------------------------------------------------------------
This one bothered me for a long time: the game's directional light
illuminated everything, but there was never actually a sun up there.
Now every sector has a visible star — a bright core wrapped in a soft
glow, anchored to the world so it holds its place in the sky while you
fly. Planets, stations and ships pass in front of it and eclipse it
naturally.

And not just any sun. Every star gets its own color, seeded into the
universe when it's created and saved with it — any hue, any saturation,
brightness up to 2x HDR. Warm white dwarfs, cold blue giants, angry
red suns, and the occasional psychedelic green. Each system's lighting
takes on its star's color, so every sector you jump into genuinely
feels different.

Hand-crafted scenarios get the same treatment automatically: anywhere
the original authors painted a sector's light color (Uncharted Space
has yellow, pink and blue systems hiding in it), that sector now shows
a matching star with zero edits required. And if you're building your
own universes, the system directional light value will tint your start as
you want it, or use the godmode sector screen's new Sun HSV
sliders to tune the current system live — hue, saturation, brightness,
with Randomize re-rolling the whole sky.

One more touch: fly into a gas cloud or dusty asteroid belt and it now
blocks 80% of the starlight. It's a giant ball of gas/dust — of course it
does.

PLANETS HAVE REAL ATMOSPHERES
------------------------------------------------------------
The old atmosphere effect was a half ported uniform rim glow — pretty,
but it shone just as brightly on the night side, which made no sense.
The new atmosphere scatters properly: blazing on the day side,
burning orangealong the terminator line, and falling away to darkness
behind. The planet surfaces' own rim lighting follows the same rule,
and every atmosphere in the system tracks the same star you see in the
sky.

THE CAMERA LEARNS TO ORBIT
------------------------------------------------------------
The free camera (unlocked, not tracking a target) used to be stuck at
a fixed tilt. Now it behaves like the cinematic camera: drag to swing
all the way around your ship on a sphere — over the top, underneath,
pitch up to ±75 degrees — with your ship pinned center-frame. Locked
camera modes are untouched.

REFLECTIONS THAT REFLECT SPACE
------------------------------------------------------------
Ships and stations now carry real-time reflections of what's actually
around them: the starfield, the local sun, distant planets — captured
by a reflection probe that sees only deep-space objects, so you never
get weird self-reflections. It updates every second as you fly, and
it's tuned separately for mobile (64px) and desktop (128px).

MOBILE: THE MAP WORKS NOW
------------------------------------------------------------
Selecting things on the system map was broken on phones — two separate
bugs stacked on top of each other (a zeroed touch-drag threshold eating
taps, and touch positions reading as (0,0) through the input layer).
Both are fixed, which also repairs tap-to-target in the 3D view and the
scratchcard minigame. Selecting a unit on the map now also plays the
target beep like it should.

THE MENU IS ALIVE
------------------------------------------------------------
The old menu was a diorama: same station, same shuttle, same sky,
forever. Now every visit rolls a brand-new system and drops the
camera into it:

- Fresh sky every time: new starfield, new star color, new exposure,
  and nebulae that finally come in every color the engine owns.
  Nebula colors roll on a rarity ladder - most skies get a few,
  and one visit in ten goes full rainbow.
- A guaranteed planet with GUARANTEED MOONS. Moon count rolls its
  own rarity ladder (1 always, up to 8 for the luckiest visits),
  each moon a different size, parked beside the disc where the
  camera can actually see it.
- Asteroid belts most visits, filled with REAL mineable rocks -
  and a refinery parked nearby so the miners have somewhere to
  sell. Every rock gets 1-2 miners flying in from the belt's
  edge, so the gold rush starts staggered, not all at once.
  The decorative rocks in belts now slowly tumble, and some
  belts are dusty while others stay clean.
- Twenty station types, one of each, spread around the system -
  every station with its own faction, STOCKED shelves and a
  trade fleet running real routes that loop forever. Factions
  occasionally build NEW stations of their own, security
  patrols form organically, everyone's friendly (mostly), and
  sometimes bandits show up. When they do, EVERYONE notices.
- The opening shot follows a lone hero ship spawning out on
  the rim with a few million credits to its faction's name -
  it has to fly in before it can work.
- The camera got curious: it jumps around the system on its
  own, preferring ships that are actually DOING something -
  mining, trading, fighting - over parked ones. Swaps hide
  behind a quick black fade, the camera holds each target for
  a proper dwell, and its orbit eases smoothly instead of
  lurching, with pitch locked to a tidy band.
- The menu's star system is now its own dedicated sector asset,
  fully decoupled from anything the battle scenarios use.

SHIELDS SHOW THEIR HEALTH
------------------------------------------------------------
Shield impacts now wear the shield section's health - the exact
same colors as the HUD widgets, always at full saturation. A hit
flashes in with a quick scale-up and an HDR overdrive of the
section's color, settles back, and the health color lingers on the
hull for a few seconds as it fades. Rapid hits each get their own
flash; a laser held on target flashes once and behaves. And when a
section finally breaks, the shield pops a hard bright red and is
gone - you'll know the exact moment a facing dies.


GRAPHICS LADDERS, TUNED
------------------------------------------------------------
- Shadows: Normal, High and Highest now reach across the ENTIRE
  sector (3000u), while keeping the same crisp near-field Low
  always had. Lowest tier remains shadows-off for the potato
  brigade.
- Post-processing: the ACES tonemap and exposure grade now run at
  EVERY quality level (they're nearly free and they're the look).
  Bloom is what low tiers give up instead - off below High.
- The sun itself scales with its star's intensity: dim stars are
  small discs, bright ones loom (1-3 brightness, 0.7-1.6x size).
- Every texture in the game now has mipmaps enabled.

A WORMHOLE TOO FAR, UNLOCKED
------------------------------------------------------------
The second universe scenario shipped complete but hidden: missing
from the universe list and locked behind an alpha-era
"temporarily unavailable" gate. Both removed. It's in the New Game
menu where it always belonged.

BUG THAT TOOK SO FUCKING LONG TO FIND AND MURDER
------------------------------------------------------------
Ships intermittently rendered solid white. The investigation went
through mipmaps, streaming budgets, materials and stars before the
culprit confessed: the reflection probe was capturing an empty sky
too early in the load and feeding it to every smooth hull. The probe
now waits for the world to be ready, captures on a fast settling
burst then a steady cadence, time-slices its work across frames, and
the sun it sees is clamped to sane brightness. Ships look like ships
again.
THIS TOOK SEVERAL FUCKING HOURS TO FIND AND I SKIPPED ON SLEEP! X3

PLOT TWIST: it came back one more time, and the TRUE final
culprit surfaced: the sun core material's color was serialized
at 1.27e+30 - an infinity-adjacent value nuking every capture
the probe ever took. Now clamped to sane values (core 2, glow 1)
and the probe's HDR capture is safely back ON.

Other notable things
------------------------------------------------------------
- The loading screen picks a random unit thumbnail every time
  (34 possible guests)
- The main menu is now randomized, it was getting pretty boring.
- The main menu has its own dedicated theme.
- The game no longer freezes when the window loses focus — the pause
  button remains the one true way to pause (PC Only).
- The version counter is now a single main-menu label driven by the
  project's own version number, with Alpha/Beta/Legacy stage tags
  ready for the road ahead.
-Wormhole jumps fully restored: the tunnel, the cap, the particles,
  the HDR glow - plus a rogue camera that used to wipe the whole
  transition to black.
- Lasers and legacy particles render properly again under URP
  (7 rebuilt shaders, 29 materials re-pointed).
- New title card on the menu - with a rainbow that slowly cycles.
- New app icon.
- The godmode sun slider's minimum brightness is now 1, matching
  the generator (no more accidentally dim stars).
- 32-bit and 64-bit builds verified for Android and Windows.
- Decorative belt rocks slowly tumble - each rock its own random
  axis, speed scaled by size (2-10 deg/s).
- Universe generation: about 2 in 3 asteroid belts are dust-free
  BY DESIGN (new dust-free belt variants), dusty types roll
  per-belt - and the sandbox "Asteroid Dust Clouds" slider now
  only affects dusty types.
- Story scenarios (Uncharted Space & co): sectors with several
  hand-placed gas clouds trimmed to one each.
- Menu belts keep a minimum separation - the same rule custom
  universes already used. No more fused double belts.
- Fixed a ghost of the old menu diorama: three leftover station
  hulks that registered as traders without ever initializing,
  crashing the menu's economy seeding.
- Universe generation hardened: one bad station's cargo seeding
  can no longer abort the whole seeding pipeline.
- Fixed menu NPCs idling forever: the "assign strategies" pass
  never actually ran (a null check against a non-nullable enum).
  Strategy-less fleets now get real work - trade runs, mining,
  patrols, even station construction.


## NOTICE FOR MIGRATING PLAYERS FROM IP2:
- Saves from before this update: their sectors keep the old near-white
  light, so their stars come from the new seeded palette on first load.
  New universes bake their stars in from the start.
- The godmode sun sliders edit the current sector only (saved with the
  game).

PLATFORM SUPPORT
------------------------------------------------------------
This release is **playable on all platforms**
Support for both 32 and 64 bit systems (Excluding linux, which is stuck to 64 only)
Runs on all devices capable of running OpenGL ES 3.1+ (Older versions will not work)
| Platform | Format | Instructions |
|----------|--------|--------------|
| **Android** | APK | Download and install directly on your device |
| **Windows** | InterstellarPilot-OpenFrontier.exe (tar.gz) | Extract the .tar.gz file and run the executable |
| **Linux** | InterstellarPilot-OpenFrontier.x86_64 (tar.gz) | Extract the .tar.gz file and run the executable |

INSTALLATION
------------------------------------------------------------
1. **Android**: Download the APK and install via your device's app manager, OR
2. **Obtainium**: Add an app, Paste the Github URL for this project as a source, set the override source to github, then press + and install.
2. **Windows/Linux**: Download the appropriate `.tar.gz` file, extract it, and run the executable
